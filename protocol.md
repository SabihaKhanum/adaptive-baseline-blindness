# Protocol: when does recalibration blind a streaming anomaly detector to persistent shifts, and how do you avoid it?

Status: pre-registration draft, revision 6 (confirmed by the author: the H1a' endpoint, H1e, K = 1000 as primary gate, the variance statistic as exploratory, the BTC illustration deferred, and H2b unchanged). Everything below is fixed before the test run. Anything changed after the freeze goes in the deviation log at the end.

## 0. Key idea and definitions

A persistent shift is both an anomaly and the new normal. The right behaviour is **alert, hold, then re-baseline**. An adaptive baseline does the last step without the first two, so it can absorb the shift before the detector has finished reacting. The remedy must keep the benefit of adaptation: it must not raise alarms when the data drifts benignly.

* **Alert state.** CUSUM and EWMA accumulators persist: they are not reset on an alarm. The detector is "in alert" at every bar where S > h (CUSUM) or |e| exceeds its limit (EWMA). Only periodic(W, R) resets accumulators, at each recompute. Flags separated by 5 bars or fewer merge into one **alert episode**. A persistent shift therefore gives one long episode, not repeated short ones.
* **First episode.** The first alert episode of an event is the first one that *starts* at or after onset. Flags belonging to an episode already in progress at onset are ignored. The rate of such contaminated events is reported, and a sensitivity analysis excludes them.
* **Blind** = no flag in the horizon, or the first episode lasts fewer than D_min bars. D_min = 10 bars (10 minutes at 1m) is the primary setting; 5 and 30 are sensitivity checks. Because accumulators persist, D_min will rarely bind for CUSUM, so **alert duration is reported directly** and not only through P(blind).
* **Shortened alert** = alert coverage (fraction of the horizon bars in alert) at most 0.5 times static's for the same detector. This is the primary endpoint of C1 (H1a'). It is defined on coverage, not on first-episode duration, so that it applies to persistent detectors (CUSUM) and intermittent ones (Isolation Forest) alike.
* **Horizon.** H = 500 bars after onset for permanent shifts, and 200 bars (the shift's lifetime) for 200-bar shifts.

## 1. Claims and hypotheses

### C1 (characterisation)

Recalibration can make a detector miss, or quickly stop flagging, a persistent shift, at matched false-alarm rate.

* **H1a (detection loss; reported whatever the outcome).** For CUSUM on the btclike stream there is at least one cell in the primary family (section 6) where P(detect) under rolling(W) or periodic(W, R = W/4) is below half of static's, with the Holm-adjusted paired test significant at 0.05. On the rev-6 dev run (30 seeds) this fails (the lowest ratio is 0.70, periodic W = 50, Holm p = 0.094; every other cell is closer to 1), so it is expected to be reported as a negative: recalibration does not stop CUSUM from detecting a persistent shift.
* **H1a' (shortened alert; the go/no-go and the primary endpoint of C1).** In the same primary family (12 cells after revision 6: level shift, delta = 1) there is at least one **shortened cell**: alert coverage under rolling(W) or periodic(W, R = W/4) is at most 0.5 times static's, with a one-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test (policy < static; ties split) Holm-adjusted over the 12 at 0.05. A sensitivity analysis excludes events contaminated for either policy. The threshold 0.5 mirrors H1a's "half of static"; it was not tuned. On the rev-6 dev run the level cells at delta = 1 with ratio <= 0.5 are rolling W = 50, 100 and periodic W = 50, 100, 250, 500 (Holm p <= 2e-5), and rolling W = 250 sits at 0.60. The secondary family (delta = 0.75, 12 comparisons) has its own Holm correction. Coverage replaces first-episode duration as the endpoint because duration is unusable for an intermittent detector such as the Isolation Forest, whose alerts are short even without adaptation.
* **H1a-practical (what the paper may claim).** A pass of H1a' only at W <= 100 supports only a claim about **toy windows** (50 to 100 minutes), and the paper says so. The practical claim, for windows in the range a pipeline would use (250 to 1,500), requires at least one Holm-significant shortened cell at W >= 250 in the primary family (level cells; the variance statistic is exploratory, section 10). Fallback ladder, fixed now:
  1. shortened cell at W >= 250 in the primary family: "practical" claim, worded "the alert is shortened", not "blind";
  2. none in the primary family, but a shortened cell at W >= 250 at delta = 0.75 (secondary family): "practical for small shifts only";
  3. otherwise: "toy windows only".
* **H1e (Isolation Forest).** For the Isolation Forest whose threshold is re-derived on the freshest quarter of its training window, alert coverage after a permanent level shift (delta in {0.75, 1}) is at most 0.5 times that of the same forest frozen at warm-up, for each training window in {500, 1000, 1500}: one-sided paired Wilcoxon, Holm over the 12 comparisons (3 training windows x 2 shift sizes x the matched reference; the unmatched reference is a sensitivity analysis). Non-inferiority of detection is reported alongside (P(detect) of the adaptive forest not lower than the frozen forest's by more than 0.10). The false-alarm matching for this detector differs from the 1-per-1,000 rule (section 3).
* **H1b (dependence on W).** P(detect) rises with W. Tested as a trend, one-sided, per shift type and for rolling and periodic separately: (i) a logistic GEE, logit P(detect) ~ log W, exchangeable correlation clustered by seed (a population-averaged stand-in for the seed random effect; used because it needs no random-effects fitting), and (ii) a within-seed permutation test on sum over seeds and W of (log W - mean log W) x outcome, permuting outcomes across W within each seed (replaces Jonckheere-Terpstra; well defined even when a seed's outcomes are all equal, which is why a per-seed Spearman correlation is not used). The permutation test is the reference when the two disagree or the GEE fails to converge.
* **H1c (within the shift's lifetime).** A 200-bar shift and a permanent shift are identical for their first 200 bars, so P(detect within 200) cannot differ between them. The in-lifetime question is therefore tested once, on the permanent runs, truncated at 200 bars: P(blind within 200 bars) and the fraction of the 200 bars spent in alert fall as W falls (same trend test as H1b). The only difference a transient shift can add is what happens after it ends, reported as an exploratory **rebound** metric: alerts raised in the 100 bars after the shift ends, because the partly absorbed baseline now sees the return to normal as a shift.
* **H1d (periodic resets).** periodic(W, R) is worse than rolling(W) in cells where the model-stream detection time D exceeds R.

If H1a' fails (for CUSUM and for the Isolation Forest): no shortened alerts on the corrected grid. Use Fallback A or B and do not stretch claims. H1a is reported either way.

### C2 (mechanism)

A simple model predicts when and whether a rolling or periodic baseline blinds the detector, and the simulation agrees.

* **Model.** s bars after onset a fraction p = min(s/W, 1) of the window is post-shift.
  * *Level shift:* baseline mean p*delta, baseline variance 1 + p(1-p)delta^2. A shifted observation has standardised mean delta(1-p)/sd(p) and scale 1/sd(p), with sd(p) = sqrt(1 + p(1-p)delta^2).
  * *Variance x2:* baseline variance 1 + p, so a shifted observation is Gaussian with scale sqrt(2)/sqrt(1 + p) in baseline units.
* **Model stream.** Run the same detector, with the same k and calibrated h, on a Gaussian stream whose mean and scale follow that path, using the policy's update schedule (for periodic: baseline constant between recomputes, accumulators reset at each recompute, onset phase uniform over R). The Monte Carlo P(detect), P(blind) and median alert duration are the **predictions**. The deterministic detection time D from the noise-free recursion is kept only as an intuition and diagnostic, since it can only give 0 or 1.
* **What the model ignores.** It ignores the estimation noise of the baseline (the baseline mean has sd 1/sqrt(W), about 0.14 at W = 50), which matters at W = 50 to 100. The comparison is made against both arms so gaps can be attributed: model versus iid shows the effect of estimation noise (and discreteness); iid versus btclike shows the effect of non-Gaussian noise. A model stream with estimation noise added is an optional refinement and is exploratory.
* **Model-stream settings.** 4,000 Monte Carlo streams per cell, seed 2026, 300 in-control bars before onset, horizon 500. The detector thresholds are the **iid-arm calibrated** thresholds for the same policy and W (the model is Gaussian). For the variance statistic the scaling a and b are the Gaussian values E|u| = sqrt(2/pi) and sd|u| = sqrt(1 - 2/pi), and k is the iid-arm k_var. Periodic: baseline constant between recomputes, accumulators reset at each recompute, onset phase uniform over R. Predictions are made for static, rolling and periodic only (not for the gate).
* **H2b (agreement).** Predictions are computed and saved before the test run. Pass: across the primary cells, mean absolute error of **alert coverage** (the H1a' endpoint) at most 0.10 and Spearman correlation between predicted and simulated coverage at least 0.8; the same two numbers for P(blind) are a secondary readout. The worst cell is reported. Cells with W <= 100 are reported separately because of estimation noise, and cells with W > 100 separately because the model ignores long-episode behaviour under volatility clustering. **Rev 6 (made after seeing dev; read this together with the variance decision in section 10).** The primary cells for H2b are the 12 level cells (rolling and periodic, six W, delta = 1), because the variance statistic is exploratory and is not run on the test seeds. The criteria (MAE <= 0.10, Spearman >= 0.8) are unchanged. On the rev-6 dev run: coverage MAE 0.045, Spearman 0.98; P(blind) MAE 0.066, Spearman 0.88; worst coverage cell rolling W = 100 (error 0.15), worst P(blind) cell rolling W = 50 (error 0.33: simulated 0.13, model 0.46). H2b therefore passes on the level cells. Over the 24 cells of revision 5, which include the 12 variance cells, it fails (coverage MAE 0.126, P(blind) MAE 0.124), because the Gaussian model overpredicts detection of variance shifts (0.96 to 1.0 against 0.33 to 0.57 simulated). Both numbers are reported. Because restricting H2b to level cells was decided after seeing the dev result, the 24-cell result is reported alongside and the paper says so. A model stream with GARCH noise is an exploratory extension and is not used for pass/fail.
* Unit test, not a hypothesis (section 7): the expected window mean moves linearly and reaches 0.9*delta at 0.9W.

### C3 (remedy)

A drift-gated update policy (freeze the baseline for at most K bars once a change is suspected, then re-baseline) restores detection, keeps the benefit of adaptation, has false-alarm rate within tolerance, and costs fewer baseline updates than the comparator.

A one-level gate engages only after the first alarm, so with the same threshold it behaves exactly like rolling until then and cannot change P(detect) (unit-tested). It can only change what follows the alarm: alert duration, coverage and P(blind). So two variants are pre-registered:

* **gated-1L(W, K)**: freeze on an alarm episode start. An ablation, claimed on alert duration, coverage and P(blind), not on P(detect).
* **gated-2L(W, K)** (the primary gate): a more sensitive **watch** level, calibrated to 1 episode per 100 in-control bars, freezes the baseline at its pre-watch values for at most K bars. The alarm level stays at 1 per 1,000, calibrated with the gate active. Early release if the watch level has not flagged for 50 bars. At timeout, set (m, s) from the last K bars. The alarm is scored against the frozen baseline.

**Shortened cells (frozen list).** The cells used by H3a and H3g are chosen *before* the test run, so they are not selected on test outcomes. A cell (shift, W, reference policy) is a shortened cell if either the model-stream prediction or the 30 dev seeds shows alert coverage(reference) / coverage(static) <= 0.5, with the reference policy either rolling or periodic (periodic is included because it is the pipeline's actual behaviour). A gate is judged against the reference policy of its cell. The list is written to `blind_cells.json` (key `union_cov`) and committed before the test seeds are run. The earlier P(blind)-gap list (gap >= 0.25, key `union`) is kept as a sensitivity check, and H3a and H3g are also reported separately on each source's list.

* **H3a (1L).** In the shortened cells, gated-1L closes at least half of the coverage gap (static minus reference), with longer first-episode duration and higher coverage than rolling.
* **H3g (2L).** In the shortened cells, gated-2L closes at least half of the coverage gap and recovers at least 90% of static's P(detect). **K = 1000 is primary.** With horizon H = 500, a gate whose K is below H releases its hold inside the horizon, so its coverage cannot exceed about K/H: K = 250 tops out near 0.45 and cannot meet a 0.5 criterion by construction (the dev run shows exactly this), while K = 1000 covers the whole horizon. K = 250 is reported as secondary and is expected to fail this clause. Because the clause is partly definitional for K >= H, H3g is read together with H3b (false alarms), H3c (freeze cost), H3e (rebound) and H3f (benign drift), which is where a gate can actually fail.
* **H3b (false alarms).** With the gate active, the alarm rate on an independent in-control stream is within +/-20% of 1 per 1,000.
* **H3c (cost of the watch level).** In control: fraction of bars frozen, baseline updates per 1,000 bars, and effect on the alarm rate. A rough estimate (watch episodes at 1 per 100 bars, 50 quiet bars to release) is that the baseline is frozen about half the time, so this cost is expected to be visible and is reported, not assumed away. A watch-rate sensitivity run at 1 per 300 is included.
* **H3d (updates).** Baseline updates per 1,000 bars are reported for every policy. gated recomputes only when not frozen, so it is at most rolling's by construction. periodic recomputes far less often than a per-bar gate, so "equal or lower updates" is claimed against rolling and DSPOT-style only. A gate wrapped around the R = W/4 schedule is reported as the budget-matched comparison.
* **H3e (transients).** After a 200-bar shift ends, the 50-quiet-bar early release returns the gate to rolling without a rebound problem (rebound metric of section 1, exploratory).
* **H3f (benefit of adaptation).** On the benign-drift arm (section 2), with thresholds calibrated on the stationary stream, the primary metric is the **fraction of ramp-window bars in alert**, A_ramp, compared with the same fraction on a stationary in-control stream, A_ctrl (same policy, same bar positions). With persistent accumulators a static detector enters alert and stays there as one merged episode, so episodes per 1,000 bars would look low, not flooded. Alarm episodes per 1,000 bars is kept only as a secondary number. Pass: static has A_ramp at least 5 times A_ctrl (if not, the arm is not a test and is reported as such), and gated has A_ramp at most 2 times max(A_ramp of rolling, A_ctrl). Expected side finding: large-W rolling lags a ramp by about slope*W/2, so its A_ramp may rise with W; this is reported either way.

If H3g fails while H1a' holds: report characterisation and mechanism only and drop C3.

## 2. Synthetic data

* **Bars.** 1-minute, n = 20,000 (about 13.9 days), warm-up 3,000, onset uniform in bars 6,000 to 12,000. W in wall-clock terms: 50 = 50 min, 100 = 1.7 h, 250 = 4.2 h, 500 = 8.3 h, 1000 = 16.7 h, 4000 = 2.8 days.
* **btclike (primary).** r_t = sigma_t z_t, sigma_t^2 = omega + alpha r_{t-1}^2 + beta sigma_{t-1}^2, z_t standardised Student-t with unit unconditional variance. **Revision 6: two-component GARCH.** A single GARCH(1,1) could not reproduce the BTC statistics (best fit on a grid: loss 111; (q99 - q1)/IQR 4.8 against 7.0; lag-60 |r| autocorrelation 0.005 against 0.21). The generator is therefore sigma_t^2 = v1_t + v2_t, with a fast and a slow component driven by the same return: v1' = w1 + a1 r^2 + b1 v1 and v2' = w2 + a2 r^2 + b2 v2, z_t standardised Student-t(nu). Parameters: a1 = 0.12109, b1 = 0.72209, a2 = 0.022579, b2 = 0.95462, fast-component variance share 0.45961, nu = 5.2641. Because both components share the driver, the unconditional variance is not the naive sum of the two components' own means; it is computed and the intercepts rescaled to make it exactly 1 (the unrescaled value was about 7.96), and the components start at their stationary values. Persistence (spectral radius) is 0.995.
  **Matching targets (monthly means of the six monthly values, 2022-01 to 2022-06, 260,634 returns):** (q99 - q1)/IQR 6.67; (q99.9 - q0.1)/IQR 13.99; |r| autocorrelation at lag 1 0.33 and at lag 60 0.17 (lag-1 r autocorrelation 0.02 and excess kurtosis 26 are reported, not fitted). The pooled values (7.04, 15.1, 0.36, 0.21) are inflated by month-to-month differences in volatility (the standard deviation of 1-minute returns varies from 0.00072 in April to 0.0013 in June), and 20,000-bar streams with unit long-run variance correspond to within-month behaviour, so the monthly means are the targets. Fit: differential evolution on the squared relative errors, validated on 10 independent streams of 400,000 bars: 6.64, 15.3, 0.355 and 0.19.
  **Known gap:** the extreme-tail ratio (q99.9 - q0.1)/IQR is about one standard deviation above its target (15.3 against 14.0), and on 43,000-bar streams its spread across streams is much larger than in the real months (sd about 8 against 1.2), because it is driven by a few extreme returns. Intraday seasonality and jumps are not modelled. The t(5) innovations alone have kurtosis 9; raw u^2 - 1 is not used (section 3).
* **iid (reference).** Gaussian, needed for the theory check. The ar1 arm is dropped to save time (a revision from the sprint doc).
* **Matching to BTCUSDT 1m.** On robust statistics: the quantile ratios (q99 - q1)/(q75 - q25) and (q99.9 - q0.1)/(q75 - q25) of returns, the autocorrelation of |r| at lags 1 and 60, and the lag-1 autocorrelation of r. Excess kurtosis is reported but is not a matching target (unstable for heavy tails). Parameters change only before the test run and are logged (revision 6).
* **Shifts.** Level +delta in unconditional sigma, delta in {0.5, 0.75, 1, 2}; variance x2 (x *= sqrt(2)). Permanent, or 200 bars (tier 2 only, for the rebound metric). delta = 0.5 equals k, so CUSUM drifts at zero rate even before absorption and comparison against static there is uninformative.
* **Benign-drift arm.** A slow ramp that should not alert: level rising +2 sigma over 5,000 bars, or variance rising from 1 to 2 over 5,000 bars, with the same onset range, held afterwards. Measured over the 5,000-bar ramp window only. Thresholds stay calibrated on the stationary stream.

## 3. Policies, detectors and statistics

* **Policies.** static; rolling(W); periodic(W, R = W/4); triggered (ADWIN or Page-Hinkley); DSPOT-style; gated-1L and gated-2L(W, K in {250, 1000}); and two comparators:
  * **lagged(W, L)**: window ending L bars ago, L in {250, 1000} to match K. Expected to delay absorption by about L bars.
  * **robust(W)**: rolling median and 1.4826 x MAD. No a priori claim that it avoids blindness: for a level shift the mixture median is halfway at p = 0.5 but reaches 90% of the new level only at p of about 0.89 (delta = 0.75 to 1) and 0.85 (delta = 2), about the same as the mean (0.90). It is reported as a comparator, with its own model-stream prediction.
* **W grid.** {50, 100, 250, 500, 1000, 4000}. W = 50 and 100 are toy windows used to verify the mechanism where theory predicts an effect; the deployment-relevant range is 250 to 1,500. CUSUM blindness at delta = 1 is expected mainly at small W, so the pre-registered delta = 0.75 (long D) probes the larger windows.
* **Detectors.** Rolling z-score; EWMA (lambda = 0.1); CUSUM; Isolation Forest (30-bar features; trained on the last `train` rows with `train` in {500, 1000, 1500}; refit every `train`/4 rows; threshold = the 1st percentile of the out-of-sample scores of the freshest `train`/4 rows, as in the pipeline). Primary: CUSUM level statistic for C1 to C3 and the theory; the Isolation Forest for H1e. The variance statistic is run on the dev seeds only (exploratory appendix).
* **Level shifts.** The statistic is u_t, with CUSUM k = 0.5.
* **Variance shifts.** The raw u^2 - 1 is not used. The statistic is y_t = (|u_t| - a)/b, where a and b are the mean and sd of |u| over the warm-up. A log(u^2 + eps) statistic is a sensitivity check. k and h are in y units. A x2 variance shifts y by only about 0.55 sd under Gaussian noise and less under btclike, so k = 0.5 would sit at or above the shift. k_var = 0.5 x (the measured shift in y), measured on dev seeds with a static baseline and frozen before the test run.
* **Calibration.** Per detector x policy x noise arm, bisect the threshold to 1 alarm episode per 1,000 in-control bars with the policy active (for gated-2L: watch level first, then alarm level with the gate active). The calibration set is **120 independent in-control streams of 20,000 bars, each with its own 3,000-bar warm-up** (seeds 12345 to 12464), not one 300,000-bar stream: the static baseline's warm-up error is shared by every bar of a stream, so the number of independent units is the number of streams. Episodes count from bar 4,600 of each stream. Pass: measured rate within 20% of target on the calibration set and on an independent check set of 120 streams (seeds 54321 to 54440). The standard error of each measured rate, clustered by stream, is reported (it replaces the 5,000-bar block bootstrap). With 40 streams, 8 of 74 iid cells missed the tolerance on the check set by sampling noise alone; with 120 none did, hence 120.
  **In-alert diagnostic.** Because accumulators persist, matching the episode rate does not match the fraction of in-control bars spent *in alert*. That fraction is reported for every cell, and cells above 5% are flagged "long episodes". It is a diagnostic only: thresholds are not adjusted to match it. On the rev-6 dev run it is 0.3% to 3.9% for the level statistic and 18% to 60% for the variance statistic with static or rolling baselines under btclike noise (see section 10).
  **Isolation Forest false-alarm matching (deviation from the 1-per-1,000 rule).** A quantile of only `train`/4 out-of-sample scores cannot go below their minimum, so this threshold mechanism cannot reach 1 alarm episode per 1,000 bars: on btclike dev streams the in-control rate is about 3 to 9 episodes per 1,000 bars for quantiles between 0 and 0.01, and 8 to 13 at the pipeline's 0.01 on the rev-6 generator (6% to 8% of in-control bars in alert). The adaptive forest therefore runs at the pipeline's q = 0.01, and the static forest (same training window, frozen at warm-up) is calibrated to the same measured in-control episode rate ("matched"); it is also reported at q = 0.01 ("unmatched"). The Isolation Forest is compared only with its own static counterpart, never with CUSUM on detection or coverage. Episodes are counted from the first scored bar, on `--m` + `--m` in-control streams of 20,000 bars (20 + 20 in the dev run; the matching is relative, not absolute, and a forest costs about 5 s per stream).

## 4. Metrics (per event)

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| P(detect) | any flag, from an episode that starts at or after onset, within the horizon |
| Restricted-mean delay | bars to first flag, misses censored at the horizon |
| Alert duration | length of the first alert episode, censored at the horizon (reported directly) |
| Alert coverage | fraction of horizon bars in alert |
| P(blind) | no flag, or first episode shorter than D_min |
| Time-to-absorption | first bar where the baseline mean (level) or scale (variance) has moved 90% of the way to the new level |
| Baseline updates per 1,000 bars | recomputations of (m, s); Isolation Forest refits reported separately |
| Fraction of bars frozen | gated policies |
| Fraction of ramp-window bars in alert | benign-drift arm (primary for H3f); episodes per 1,000 bars secondary |
| Rebound alerts | alerts in the 100 bars after a 200-bar shift ends |

The same seed gives the same stream for every policy and detector (each stream is generated once and reused), so comparisons are paired. Test seeds are not touched during development.

**Tiers and stop rule.** The full grid is about 290,000 streams per detector, and the gated, robust and DSPOT policies are stateful per-bar loops. So the test run is organised in tiers, run in this order, and if time runs out the lowest tier is dropped, and the paper says so:

* **Tier 1:** the primary family (CUSUM, btclike, permanent level shift delta = 1, static, rolling and periodic, six W values; the variance statistic is not run on the test seeds); gated-1L and gated-2L (K = 1000 primary, K = 250 secondary); the benign-drift arm (level ramp only) for static, rolling and gated-2L; the iid arm for the theory check; for the vectorised policies only, the other shift sizes (0.5, 0.75, 2); and the Isolation Forest check H1e (seeds 101 to 200; about 15 s of single-core time per stream for the three windows).
* **Tier 2:** lagged, robust, DSPOT-style, EWMA and z-score, and the 200-bar transient shifts (rebound metric).
* **Tier 3:** triggered, and Isolation Forest seeds 201 to 300.

Before the test run, one stream per policy is timed and the timings are logged; the per-bar policies use numba or joblib. A tier is started only if its projected time fits the remaining compute budget (budget to be recorded at freeze: 4 hours hours of wall-clock time, 14 logical cores). With the numba implementation, measured on one cloud container core: calibrating one arm (74 cells, 120 + 120 streams) takes about 2 minutes, and a 30-seed dev run over all Tier 1 policies and W takes about 10 seconds, so Tier 1 over 200 test seeds is expected to take minutes; the stop rule is expected to bind only for Tier 3. Claims that depend on a dropped tier are not made.

## 6. Analysis plan

1. **Calibration table** per detector x policy x noise arm. A cell outside +/-20% is flagged and excluded from claims.
2. **Figure 1** (dev seeds): fraction flagging at each bar after onset, static versus rolling(W), for permanent +1 sigma and +2 sigma.
3. **Go/no-go** (30 dev seeds): H1a' on dev seeds (H1a is reported alongside). Proceed only if H1a' holds. The shortened-cell list and k_var are fixed here.
4. **Primary family.** CUSUM, btclike, permanent level shift delta = 1, rolling and periodic versus static across the six W values: 2 x 6 = 12 paired comparisons (24 in revision 5, which included the variance statistic; dropping it after the dev run makes the Holm correction less strict, which is stated here; the dev p-values of the level cells are below 2e-5, so the conclusion does not depend on it). Primary test (H1a'): one-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank on alert coverage, with the coverage ratio and its paired bootstrap 95% interval (10,000 resamples), Holm over the 12. Detection loss (H1a): exact McNemar on the paired detect indicators, same family and correction. The delta = 0.75 secondary family (rolling and periodic versus static, six W: 12 comparisons) has its own Holm correction, and H1e has Holm over its 6 comparisons. 7. **Gating experiments (Figure 4):** Pareto plot of alert coverage (and P(blind)) against false-alarm rate and baseline updates per 1,000 bars for gated-1L, gated-2L, rolling, periodic, DSPOT-style, lagged and robust, at matched false-alarm rate; the benign-drift arm (H3f) and the in-control freeze cost (H3c).

## 7. Unit tests (not hypotheses)

* In-control streams have the right mean, variance and autocorrelation; btclike has the target quantile ratios and |r| autocorrelation.
* Baselines are causal (changing future bars does not change earlier (m, s)).
* The expected window mean after onset is linear and reaches 0.9*delta at 0.9W.
* gated-1L and rolling give identical flags up to the first alarm, for every seed, **when both use the same threshold (the one calibrated for rolling)**. The gated-1L threshold calibrated separately may differ and is not used in this test.
* The vectorised and bar-by-bar paths agree.
* Model-stream predictions reproduce the known CUSUM visibility limits (p of about 0.44 at delta = 1 and 0.66 at delta = 2), and the mixture-median absorption points (p of about 0.89 at delta = 1 and 0.85 at delta = 2).
* The first-episode rule ignores an episode in progress at onset; the implementation is checked against a plain-python reference on 600 random alarm arrays, and `contaminated` is reported per event. `detect_raw` (any flag in the horizon) is kept for comparison, and the go/no-go is also run excluding events contaminated for either policy.
* The model stream reproduces the expected orderings (static detects a +2 sigma shift essentially always; rolling and periodic at W = 50 detect less than static at +1 sigma).
* The ramp runner (H3f) shows a static baseline in alert for most of a +2 sigma ramp and a rolling W = 250 baseline mostly not.
* The in-control in-alert fraction of the level statistic is below 5% on iid noise.
* The shortened-cell test flags a synthetic cell with coverage ratio 0.2 and not one with ratio 0.96; the model stream's coverage for rolling W = 50 is below static's.

## 8. Outcomes and what is claimed

| Result | Claim |
|---|---|
| H1a' at W >= 250 (level cells), H2b, H3g (K = 1000) and H3f hold | Full paper: C1 to C3, with the practical claim "the alert is shortened" (C4 deferred) |
| H1a' only at W <= 100 (or only delta = 0.75 at W >= 250) | The paper states "toy windows only" (or "small shifts only"); C3 still tested |
| H1a' holds, H3g fails | Characterisation and mechanism only; drop C3 and say so |
| H1a' holds, H2b fails (on the level cells) | Characterisation only; the simple model does not explain it |
| H1a' fails | Fallback A or B; no shortened-alert claim |
| H1e holds | The Isolation Forest result is reported as its own finding (re-derived thresholds absorb a persistent shift) |
| A tier is dropped | Claims depending on it are not made; the paper says so |

H1a (detection loss) is reported whichever way it falls: a negative H1a is part of the claim, namely that detection is preserved while the alert is shortened.

## 9. Provenance and freeze

* These hypotheses were motivated by earlier exploratory results on BTC data: that periodic, graded and uniform schedules were indistinguishable, and that the Isolation Forest threshold, re-derived on the freshest quarter, adapted quickly. They were not tested confirmatively on those data.
* The H1a' endpoint (coverage), the hypothesis H1e, K = 1000 as primary gate and the Isolation Forest matching rule were chosen in revision 5 after looking at dev seeds 1 to 30 for CUSUM and for the Isolation Forest. The test seeds (101 and up) were not touched.
* Freeze: tag both the protocol and the simulator at the same commit as `prereg-v1`, after the simulator is complete, k_var and the btclike parameters are set, and `blind_cells.json` exists, and before any test seed is run. The commit hash is recorded in `FREEZE.md`, committed after the tag, since a file cannot contain the hash of its own commit.

## 10. Known limits

* The synthetic stream is a model of BTCUSDT 1m, not the data. Shifts are injected, so onsets are known by construction.
* The theory is Gaussian and ignores baseline estimation noise; the iid and btclike arms attribute the gaps.
* delta is in units of unconditional sigma; under volatility clustering the local sigma varies with regime at onset.
* The Isolation Forest adapts through its own threshold re-derivation, a different mechanism from the baseline policies, so it is tested separately (H1e) and not fitted to the model. At the pipeline's quantile it runs at roughly ten times CUSUM's in-control episode rate (5% to 7% of bars in alert), and it is compared only with its own frozen counterpart.
* D_min = 10, the btclike parameters, the coverage threshold 0.5, and the choice of gated-2L with K = 1000 as the primary gate are the author's choices, confirmed for revision 6.
* **Variance statistic (decision, revision 6): exploratory, dev seeds only.** Under the rev-6 btclike noise a static baseline is in alert for 57% to 60% of in-control bars and rolling baselines for 19% to 45% at the matched 1 episode per 1,000 bars, and gated-2L is frozen 72% to 99% of the time (so it acts as a static baseline). Many variance events therefore start with an alert already in progress, and on the variance ramp the static detector is in alert 53% of the time even in control, so H3f is not a test for variance. The variance statistic is not run on the test seeds and appears only as an exploratory appendix from the dev run.
* **gate rebound.** A gate with K longer than a transient shift re-baselines on a window that is mostly shift bars, which can create a rebound alert after the shift ends (exploratory metric, section 1).
* **Model agreement.** On the rev-6 dev run the Gaussian model agrees with the simulation on the level cells (coverage MAE 0.045, Spearman 0.98) and does not on the variance cells (it overpredicts variance-shift detection); the 24-cell figure (MAE 0.126) is reported alongside. The largest level-cell gaps are at small W, where clustered volatility lengthens real alerts beyond the iid prediction (rolling W = 50: simulated first alert 44 bars, model 18).
* **Gate cost.** Gated-2L is frozen 37% to 51% of in-control bars (level statistic) and updates about 500 to 630 times per 1,000 bars, against about 6% frozen and 940 updates for gated-1L. It still tracks a benign level ramp (H3f holds on dev). The trade-off is reported, not hidden.
* **Gate and horizon.** The coverage criterion for a gate depends on K relative to the horizon H = 500; K = 1000 holds the alert for 16.7 hours before re-baselining, which is an operating choice and not a free parameter. The 500-bar coverage metric cannot distinguish holding from holding and then re-baselining; the re-baselining step is tested only by the exploratory rebound metric (H3e) and the benign-drift arm (H3f).
